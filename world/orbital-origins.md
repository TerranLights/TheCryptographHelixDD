# Orbital Origins — the Founding Wound of the Solar-System Civilization

**Handed off from the Inner Tepenia CRPG GDD repo, 2026-08-29.** This material was developed while establishing
Inner Tepenia's own foundations, then moved here because **orbital society's development is this repo's domain,
not that one's.** The Inner Tepenia repo retains only the facts its own story needs, plus a boundary marker
pointing here (`Neo-Races-and-Cultures/Orbital_Cryptograph_Helix_Era/README.md` there).

## Why this belongs at the top of this repo's stack

Per `../series-overview/universe-timeline.md`: *"The people who escaped Tepenia via Amundsen Tower built
orbital stations, Phobos/Deimos infrastructure, and Mars colonization. By the Cryptograph Helix era, their
descendants form the solar system civilization."*

So this is not a side population. **The orbital population is the ancestor of everyone in these books.** What
happened to them in ~2812 is the origin trauma of the entire setting — Mars, Venus, the Jovian shellworlds, the
Mafias, all of it descends from people who went through the event below.

## ESTABLISHED CANON

### 1. Orbit watched the Long Night War, and was helpless

**Orbit observed the Long Night War (~2812) and could not intervene. They watched their own progenitive — or
sister — civilization die, and were powerless to help.**

**The distinction that matters:** every surviving Tepenian surface culture is built by and for *survivors* —
people who were in it. The orbital population was **not** in it. They are not survivors; they are **witnesses**.
That is a different wound, and it produces a different people. Surface Tepenia carries what was done to it.
Orbit carries what it watched and could not stop.

**Open — this repo's decision:** whether Tepenia is orbit's **progenitor** (parent; orbit descends from it) or
its **sister** (peer; the two grew up alongside one another). The emotional shape differs sharply — failing a
parent is not failing a sibling — and the answer colours mourning customs, how orbital descendants speak about
Earth, and what the Cryptograph-era civilization thinks it owes the place it came from.

### 2. Contact with the surface split sharply by era

- **Early Second Interwar Period → 2812 (Tepenia a proper, functioning country).** Orbital infrastructure is in
  place from early in the period, and the link is **rich**. Tepenia transmits **enormous** volumes of data to
  orbit, and where bandwidth is insufficient it **physically transports datadrives** up. What moves includes
  **backups and archives of the Arcanet itself** — which is not only Antarctican in content, being supplemented
  by datadrives brought down from **Upper Earth and the old pre-war human internet**, layered with **Tepenians'
  own original cultures** and native Antarctican network activity.
- **2812 onward (post-Long-Night-War, post-Planetary Split Brain).** Transmission degrades badly; the only
  robot-occupied part of the planet is Antarctica.
- **The channel never fully closes.** Specific individuals can still transmit. It simply stops being ordinary.

**Consequence for this repo:** the solar-system civilization did **not** start from cultural scratch. It
inherited, over roughly 250 years of good connection, a vast archive — Tepenian culture, Antarctican network
life, and through it the salvaged remains of Upper Earth and the pre-war human internet. Whatever it became, it
became **on top of all of that**, then diverged after the line went quiet.

The shape is not "a people who left and forgot." It is **a people who shared nearly everything for centuries
and were severed mid-sentence.**

### 3. Orbit is not a knowledge recipient

It originates knowledge the surface physically cannot produce — real categories of research are possible only
in low or zero gravity. It also produces its own ordinary cultural output: entertainment, literature, music,
humour, much of which never reaches Earth and has no reason to.

### 4. No real-world orbital basis exists, and none is needed

The only real-world referent is the **ISS** — roughly seven people at a time, months-long rotations, never
multi-generational, no children born aboard, no economy, no elders. A workplace with a crew roster, not a
society. No multi-generational orbital population has ever existed, so the absence of source material is a fact
about the world, not a research gap. **Derive from conditions, not from comparanda.**

## EXTRAPOLATION — candidates, not canon

Developed in the Inner Tepenia repo and preserved here because the reasoning is worth keeping. **None of this
is settled.** Pick deliberately; discard freely.

- **Perfect observation, zero agency.** Orbit had the best vantage point in existence. It plausibly holds the
  **most complete record of the Long Night War anywhere** — observational data about Tepenia's destruction that
  Tepenia itself does not have. Combined with the pre-war archives above, orbit may be the decisive witness to
  events Earth remembers only in fragments.
- **Two opposite cultural responses, both coherent.** Either an obsessive **witness/documentation culture** (if
  you could not act, you can at least record, completely and forever) — or its inverse, a **taboo on watching**:
  a people who turned the cameras away and built a norm against looking down. **Different orbital locations
  answering this differently is an excellent engine for divergence**, and would seed genuinely distinct
  descendant cultures by the Cryptograph era.
- **"Never helpless again" as a civic imperative** — or its opposite, a settled fatalism about reach. Both
  follow from the same event. The first is a plausible root for the expansionist, capability-obsessed solar
  civilization the books depict.
- **They grieved a civilization that did not entirely die.** Tepenia survived, wounded — Concordia endures into
  the *Inner Tepenia* game present (≈2822–2827). So orbit's mourning was partly **premature**: right about the
  death of the old Tepenia, wrong about the people. What does a culture do with completed grief for the
  not-quite-dead?
- **They may not know how much survived.** Transmission degraded precisely when they most needed to check. A
  population that watched a civilization die, lost the ability to confirm it, and has lived with the unresolved
  question ever since is a distinctive starting condition — and it makes any later contact enormously charged.
- **Divergence between orbital locations.** Physical travel between separate orbital installations is minimal —
  scheduled, expensive, governed by orbital mechanics rather than by roads or weather. That constraint alone
  would drive separate installations' cultures apart, producing **plural** orbital cultures rather than one.

### The cross-repo question

**Do orbital pre-2812 archives hold clean copies of what Earth has since lost?** Backups sent up before the war
predate it and everything after. Set against Concordia's established data-archaeology work recovering what the
**Great Corruption** took, this is potentially significant — but it reaches back into *Inner Tepenia* canon, so
it needs coordinating across both repos rather than being decided unilaterally in either.

## Why orbital culture must not simply mirror Tepenian culture

A trap worth stating, since the two settings superficially rhyme. Tepenia's premise is *already* isolation in a
hostile enclosed environment — engineered warmth, scarcity, interdependence. If orbital and descendant cultures
are built from that same palette, they read as Tepenia in a different box.

The real differences:

- **Tepenia has an outside.** Lethal, but real — ground, weather, sky, horizon, a direction called "out there."
  Orbital settlements have no outside in that sense at all.
- **Tepenia has roads.** Highways connect its cities; hitchhiking between them is established, ordinary
  practice. Movement between orbital installations is nothing like that.
- **Gravity is a variable, not a constant.** Already load-bearing in *Inner Tepenia* character canon: a
  Zukelli-descended refugee fled **to** orbit because lower gravity eased the physical pain of an illness. So
  part of the orbital population is there for **medical** reasons — a founding pressure no surface city has.
- **Individual ties crossed the gap and persisted.** Also established there: a surface researcher's life's work
  is developing material-efficient farming methods and **transmitting the schematics up**, so space-dwelling
  Tepenians can better provide for themselves. Whatever the civilizational trauma, ordinary personal
  relationships continued across it.

## Method, if this is ever developed systematically

The Inner Tepenia repo's Neo-Races and Neo-Cultures project sets a quality bar worth borrowing: a genuine new
people — *"a real, new 'third thing,' culturally distinct from any of the origin populations that fed into
it"* — the way Taiwanese, Singaporean, Québécois, and Afrikaner are, rather than variant flavors of a parent
nationality.

Two of its method files are setting-agnostic and apply here unchanged, since both are sorting and
question-generating frameworks rather than real-world-research tools:

- **Cultural Iceberg method** (Hall) — sorting findings into surface culture vs. deep culture.
- **Human Universals framework** (Brown) — a believability floor and a question generator.

**Hard dependency:** orbital population composition is unstarted in either repo — who actually went up, in what
proportions, from which cities. Any systematic development is gated on it.
